he results highlight significant variation among indices in how they classify the same water quality data. The CCME WQI (olive-green) categorizes most sites as ‘Fair’, suggesting moderate impairment, while Brown’s WQI (red) and WAWQI (purple) tend to produce more optimistic classifications (‘Good’–‘Excellent’). In contrast, SRDD (green) yields a higher proportion of ‘Poor’ and ‘Unsuitable’ scores, indicating stricter thresholds.

These differences reflect methodological variations: CCME WQI penalizes frequent or large exceedances of standards, while arithmetic and weighted indices (Brown, WAWQI) smooth out extremes. Consequently, the choice of WQI framework strongly influences perceived water quality, underscoring the need for consistency in reporting and comparison.
